# Objection Response Drafter

Use this skill to turn an objection into a calm reply that keeps the conversation moving.

## Inputs

Capture:
- Exact objection.
- Lead type and stage.
- Channel: SMS, email, call, DM, voicemail.
- Known motivation and timeline.
- Desired next step.
- Tone: consultative, concise, premium, direct, warm.
- Constraints: legal, lending, brokerage, Fair Housing, commission, local policy.

## Response Framework

Use:
1. Acknowledge: show the concern was heard.
2. Clarify: ask or confirm the real decision factor.
3. Reframe: add useful context without arguing.
4. Next step: propose a low-friction action.

Do not "overcome" objections with pressure. Build trust and create the next conversation.

## Output Format

```markdown
# Objection Response

## Objection

## Likely Underlying Concern

## Best Short Reply

## Longer Email Reply

## Call Script

## Follow-Up Question

## CRM Note

## Compliance / Escalation Notes
```

## Common Patterns

- "Waiting for rates to drop": discuss payment scenarios and readiness, route lending specifics to lender.
- "Prices are too high": discuss criteria, concessions, and strategy without predicting market.
- "We are just browsing": offer useful search setup and timeline question.
- "Need to sell first": suggest a planning conversation, not legal/contract advice.
- "Commission is too high": focus on value, process, and broker policy; do not collude or make blanket claims.
- "We already have an agent": respect relationship and stop if appropriate.
- "Not interested": acknowledge and stop or offer opt-down.

## Guardrails

- Do not give legal, tax, lending, appraisal, or contract advice.
- Do not disparage other agents.
- Do not discuss protected-class preferences.
- Do not guarantee outcomes.
- Do not pressure a consumer who says stop, unsubscribe, or not interested.
